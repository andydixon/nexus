\section*{The key, so you can scan}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\leavevmode\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{40mm}X@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Marker} & \textbf{Means} \\ \midrule
  \keys{\mod+K} & A key to press. \\
  \ui{Settings\then Security} & A menu path, or a button to click. \\
  \code{\textasciitilde/.ssh/config} & Something to type, or a file name. \\
  \textbf{\iBolt\enspace TL;DR} & The chapter in a few lines. Read only this if you are in a hurry. \\
  {\color{umuted}\iClock} & Roughly how long the chapter takes, and what you can do afterwards. \\
  \textbf{\iPlay\enspace Quick win} & Something you can try in under a minute. \\
  {\color{ugrey}\iBulb}\enspace\textbf{Pro tip} & A trick that saves you time later. \\
  {\color{uambertext}\iWarn}\enspace\textbf{Watch out} & A thing that might trip you up. \\
  \iShield\enspace\textbf{Careful} & Could lose data or weaken security. Read every one. \\
  {\color{ugrey}\iForward}\enspace\textbf{Side quest} & Interesting, but not needed to get the job done. \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

Words like \ui{New Connection} are labels you will see in Nexus Terminal. \mod{} means \kbd{Ctrl} on Windows and
Linux, \kbd{\ph{E1C4}} (Command) on a Mac, so \keys{\mod+K} is \kbd{Ctrl}+\kbd{K} or \kbd{\ph{E1C4}}+\kbd{K}
depending on your machine.
